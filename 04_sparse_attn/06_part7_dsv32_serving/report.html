The claim this part establishes: for DeepSeek-V3.2 in its official DP8+EP8 layout, decode is power-flat (~969 W/GPU regardless of batch), so tokens/joule scales almost linearly with concurrency. The only resource that limits concurrency is KV-cache capacity — not memory bandwidth. A capacity-extended HBM at unchanged bandwidth converts directly into throughput and energy efficiency.

§1Simulation pipeline — from Part VI's measured kernels to cluster metrics

Nothing here is analytical guesswork: every latency the simulator emits is a lookup (or a documented extrapolation) into the H200 kernel measurements behind Part VI.

  • Profiles: the Part-VI profiling run measured one full V3.2 decoder layer (DSA attention wrapper incl. lightning indexer + FlashMLA, MoE 256-expert block, dense/per-sequence ops) on an H200 at tp1, over a 4D attention grid reaching n_decode = 1024 and kv = 1M (10,483 attention points, zero failed shots).
  • Simulator: LLMServingSim replays a vLLM-style continuous-batching scheduler (chunked prefill, block KV allocator, admission control) and prices every iteration from those profiles; energy comes from its per-second power model.
  • DP8+EP8 as an EP8-rank proxy: the 8 DP ranks are symmetric, so one rank is simulated as a single tp1 instance of a 32-expert slice model (256/8 experts — exactly what one EP rank holds; weights verified byte-identical, 97.66 GB). A cluster batch B runs as b = B/8 requests on the rank; cluster throughput = 8×rank, J/token = rank value. The EP all-to-all (~0.9 MB/direction/iteration at decode) is the one un-modeled term.
  • Custom-HBM axis: per-GPU npu_mem ∈ {141 (stock H200), 256, 512, 1024} GiB at fixed 4.8 TB/s. This is the "same bandwidth, bigger capacity" abstraction of the HBM + LPDDR-extension architecture under study.

§2Per-GPU memory map — why capacity binds

Under DP, every rank keeps the full KV of its own sessions (MLA latents are TP-replicated and DP-partitioned, never sharded), so per-GPU KV demand scales with batch × context.

1M does not fit on stock H200: 94.65 GiB weights + 46.94 GiB for a single 1M session = 141.6 GiB > 141 GiB. In the measured runs the scheduler could never admit the request (the simulator livelocks rather than rejecting — recorded as a timeout). Long-context V3.2 serving on this cluster requires capacity extension before any performance question even arises.

Decode energy per token (J) vs. batch @128K. Decode power is ~flat (~969 W/GPU), so J/token ∝ TPOT/b — it collapses 18.65 → 0.69 J as concurrency amortizes the weight traffic.

Why it saturates gently: TPOT grows only 4.8× over a 128× batch increase. The MoE block's expert-weight reads (the dominant memory traffic at low batch) are amortized across concurrent tokens; the per-batch costs that do grow — the indexer scan (∝ b×L) and per-token attend — are much smaller until deep into the curve.

Over-capacity requests are not served worse — they are not served concurrently at all: vLLM-style admission control queues them, so throughput hard-saturates at the wall (e.g. stock @128K tops out at the B=64 row: ~1.6k tok/s and ~5 J/token, 7× and 7× away from the B=1024 point a 1 TiB GPU reaches).

§6Measurement methodology — getting a clean steady state out of a serving simulator

  • Workload: b requests, each with input = L, arriving at t=0; chunked prefill (64K-token chunks) serializes their prefills. Request i is given output = (b−1−i)·L/64K + 64 tokens, so every request finishes its ramp exactly when the last prefill ends — all b then decode their final 64 tokens at full concurrency and complete together.
  • Steady TPOT = the last-prefilled request's TPOT (its decode happens entirely in the aligned steady phase). Validated per run by counting requests alive in its decode window (must equal b, else the cell is reported as capacity-limited).
  • Decode J/token = mean decode-phase power (per-second samples with zero prompt throughput) × TPOT / b. Decode power is ~flat at ~969 W/GPU across all batches — the whole energy win comes from concurrency.
  • Prefix caching disabled: two reasons — identical synthetic prompts would fake-share KV across sessions, and (found during this study) the simulator's radix-tree accounting never charges KV for token-ID-less workloads, silently voiding all capacity limits.

§7Caveats

  • EP all-to-all is not modeled (single-rank proxy): at decode it is ~0.9 MB per direction per iteration per rank over NVLink — a small additive TPOT term that would shift all cells equally.
  • Contexts > 128K are hypothetical-context V3.2: RoPE is out of its trained range, but kernel latency depends only on allocated KV length, so the performance numbers remain valid as an architecture study.
  • Extrapolated corners: the attention grid was measured out to (n_decode=1024, kv≤128K) and (kv=1M, n_decode≤128); the few cells beyond both wedge edges are log-linear extrapolations of a measured-linear scan surface.
  • J/token here is decode-only; end-to-end J/token including prefill energy is in the results CSV. Cooling/PSU overheads and host DRAM/interconnect energy for spill paths are outside the model.
