DeepSeek-V3.2 — Part XI: Custom-HBM LPDDR Extension, Honestly Charged

The custom-HBM design point specified end-to-end: each GPU’s 8 HBM stacks carry a base die fronting a 128 GB/s × 128 GB LPDDR extension (per GPU: 1,024 GB/s wire aggregate, 1,024 GB capacity, 1,216 GB total). Latent KV lives in LPDDR and is gathered through the base die at an assumed 0.5 wire efficiency — b = 512 GB/s achieved scatter, a = 0.2 µs — charged with the measured, in-situ-validated window-credited tier law from Part X. Six GPU-only variants — 8-GPU stock and 8/6/4/2/1-GPU custom (no KV offload or eviction) — batch ladders under each max fit, four context lengths, in LLMServingSim over measured B200 kernels.

Part IX evaluated this capacity design with LPDDR idealized to HBM bandwidth. Part X measured what the gather actually costs and how much of it hides. This page closes the loop: the comparison, now spanning the full 8/6/4/2/1 GPU-count ladder, with the LPDDR gather charged by the validated law at the user-specified engine spec. The capacity verdict survives — and the honest model reveals structure the idealization could not: a throughput-optimal batch below the capacity ceiling at long context, and a bandwidth tax that matters most exactly where batches are largest.

§2Method — validated pieces only

  • EP-rank proxy: one simulated tp1 rank stands for each DP=EP rank; cluster batch = N×b, cluster throughput = N×rank. TPOT at equal per-rank batch is GPU-count-invariant (measured: MoE latency flat in expert count).
  • The lpddr512 patch: only the 115 pure-decode attention rows change: t_att += max( floor(N), a + N/b − W(n) ) − 2·δ_kernel(n) with N = n·2048·656 B (latent gather bytes only), W = 2·fusedk_B (measured overlap window), floor = 0.67 + N/44,642·10³ µs (measured HBM exposure floor), 2·δ_kernel = measured HBM exposed gather (subtracted to avoid double counting). Weights, indexer scan, top-k, MoE, dense: untouched measured values. The window-credited law reproduced 8 in-situ PCIe layer measurements to ≤0.28% (Part X §14).
  • Steady state: staggered-output workloads; steady TPOT = the last-prefilled request’s TPOT over its final 64 tokens at full concurrency; validity gate n_eff = b at the steady-window midpoint (50/50 cells pass); decode power = tail plateau of the per-second power series; TTFT = first request’s single-prefill latency.
  • Batch ladder: max fit + descending powers of two (≥8; down to 1 when max ≤ 8) — the menu between throughput-max and latency-min.
  • Anchors: the stock cells reproduce the Part IX runs — (14, 128K) +0.01%, (28, 64K) −0.02%.

§5Findings

  • 1 · The capacity win survives honest charging and grows with context: 2.23× (64K) → 8.55× (1M) for 8-GPU custom vs 8-GPU stock, identically for energy per token. Six custom GPUs beat eight stock ones at every context (1.66–6.28×) with 25% less silicon.
  • 2 · NEW: the throughput-optimal batch sits below the capacity fit at long context. C8 at 512K peaks at b=32 (5,191 tok/s) not at its b=44 fit (5,034); same for C6 (3,895 @ 32 vs 3,772 @ 43). The LPDDR gather tax grows ~linearly in batch while marginal throughput grows sublinearly — past the crossover, one more session costs more TPOT than it adds tokens. The flat-BW idealization (Part IX) never showed this: there, more batch was always better. Consequence: at long context, capacity beyond b* buys admission headroom and QoS slack, not throughput.
  • 3 · Iso-latency wins: even at near-stock per-user latency the custom systems beat stock — see the detailed subsection and table below.
  • 4 · The max-batch latency price: 4.4–5.6× stock’s TPOT (users read 6.5–17 tok/s vs stock’s 36–46). The ladders in §4 are the negotiation range.
  • 5 · TTFT is variant-invariant (3.4 / 7.3 / 40.8 / 143 s at 64K/128K/512K/1M) by construction — prefill is unpatched. A real LPDDR tier could slow prefill KV writes; unmodeled (§6).
  • 6 · The cost of honesty shrinks with context: vs Part IX’s flat-BW numbers, lpddr512 gives back 23% (64K), 15% (128K), 5% (512K), 2% (1M) of max-batch throughput. Gather traffic scales with batch only, while scan/attend grow with kv — so the bandwidth assumption matters most exactly where batches are largest.
  • 7 · GPU count is a linear knob down to C4, then the premise breaks: C8/C6/C4 TPOT agree at equal per-rank batch (N-invariance under the patch) and cluster throughput scales ~N/8. C2 and C1 continue the pattern numerically, but their weights no longer fit HBM (323 / 627.6 GiB vs 179), so their simulated numbers omit LPDDR weight streaming — treat them as upper bounds (§6). Capacity also compounds: free KV per GPU shrinks from 1,038 GiB (C8) to 505 GiB (C1), so per-GPU max batch halves 176 → 86 at 128K.
  • 8 · The break-even GPU count falls with context: matching 8 stock GPUs takes 4 custom GPUs at 64K–128K (1.09× / 1.35×) and only 2 at 512K–1M (1.30× / 1.93×, upper bounds); a single custom GPU reaches 0.74× of the whole 8-GPU stock cluster at 1M. Per-GPU throughput at the 128K best points is nearly flat across the custom ladder — 1,494 / 1,479 / 1,448 / 1,365 tok/s/GPU for C8/C6/C4/C2, all 2.6–2.8× stock’s 535 — while C1’s 1,186 is capacity-capped (its 627.6 GiB of weights leave room for only b=86).

Iso-latency, in detail — what survives when the custom system may not spend your latency

The max-batch comparison invites a fairness objection: stock runs at ~23–27 ms TPOT while custom runs at 59–154 ms — the throughput win is partly bought by making every user read 4–6× slower. Iso-latency ("iso" = equal) removes that trade: dial the custom system’s batch down its ladder until its TPOT returns to ≈ stock’s, then ask how much throughput advantage survives. Selection rule (mechanical, from the ladder): the lowest-TPOT ladder point whose cluster throughput still ≥ stock’s max-batch throughput. Because the ladder is discrete (batch steps of 8+), the custom point lands slightly above stock’s TPOT — the “TPOT penalty” column says by how much.

Reading it: at 128K the 8-GPU custom system matches stock’s latency within 4% and still serves 1.10× the tokens. At 512K the strictest clearing point is b=4: 1,288 tok/s at +3% TPOT = 1.29×; allowing +17% (b=8) raises it to 2.27×. At 1M the sub-8 rungs (added after review) show the cleanest result of the study: at b=1 the custom system runs at exactly stock’s 23.08 ms — the LPDDR gather is fully window-hidden — tying 1.00×, and one rung up (b=2, +8%) it serves 1.85×. 64K is the boundary case: custom only reaches parity-plus (1.05× at +9%), since short context is where stock is least capacity-limited. The design therefore does not require the max-batch latency price: the ladder (§4) is a menu whose ends are the throughput ceiling and these latency-preserving points; a deployment picks by its TPOT SLO. The C6 columns show the 6-GPU system clearing stock at iso-latency for 128K–1M (1.38–1.70× at +8–24%), though at 64K it needs high batch (84 ms) to pass stock’s throughput at all.

§6Honesty box — what these numbers assume

b = 512 GB/s is an assumed engine spec (0.5 of the 1,024 GB/s wire), not a measurement. Measured reference efficiencies for scattered 656-B gathers: TMA-copy engine ~34% of pins, transfer-only ceiling 63–74%, PCIe production kernel 16.6% of its link. 0.5 sits between DMA-copy class and the ceiling — it requires the deep-pipeline base-die engine (k_eff → 1). a = 0.2 µs is aggressive vs the measured HBM head (0.67 µs/step). The Ramulator base-die model is the tool that must certify both.

C2/C1 weight-spill warning. The gather-only patch charges latent-KV gathers to LPDDR but leaves every weight access at measured HBM speed. That premise holds for S8/C8/C6/C4 (rank weights ≤ 170.7 GiB < 179 GiB HBM) but fails for C2 (323 GiB) and C1 (627.6 GiB): most of their weights would live in LPDDR. A worst-case bound on the omitted cost — streaming min(8b, n_local)×44 MB of expert weights per MoE layer at 512 vs 8,000 GB/s — reaches tens of ms per step at high batch, i.e. potentially larger than the entire simulated TPOT. C2/C1 rows are therefore upper bounds whose realism requires either weight pinning strategies (hot experts in HBM) or a weight-tier extension of the patch (future work).

  • Keys-in-HBM flag: the indexer scan is unpatched (keys assumed HBM-resident). Cells where b × keys/session exceeds free HBM overstate this: C8 — 64K b≥256, 128K b≥128, 512K b≥32, 1M b≥16; C6 (less free HBM) — 64K b≥128, 128K b≥64, 512K b≥16, 1M b≥8. Long-context max-batch rows are therefore optimistic; the iso-latency mid-ladder rows are largely clean.
  • Decode-only patch: prefill sparse attention and KV writes keep measured HBM values (hence the variant-invariant TTFT).
  • EP all-to-all unmodeled (sub-percent at NVLink5); C6 uses the 43-expert heavy-rank proxy (≤2% conservative).
  • Dummy-weight kernel profiles; 1-layer probe lineage — same basis as Parts VI–X.

§7Evaluation: S8 vs C6 vs C4, paper-style

This section restates the study as the evaluation of an architecture paper: a fixed baseline (S8, 8× stock B200), two custom-HBM design points that both keep weights HBM-resident (C6, C4 — the honest rungs of the ladder), two contexts (128K, 1M), and four lenses: max-batch capability, iso-latency, SLO-constrained goodput, and iso-throughput energy. Absolute numbers and baseline-normalized factors are given everywhere; every value is generated from results.csv.

7.1 · Methodology

Workload: pure-decode steady state at context L; per-rank batch b swept over a ladder up to each variant’s no-eviction capacity fit. Metrics: TPOT (per-user inter-token latency; user rate = 1000/TPOT), cluster throughput = N·b/TPOT, J/token from the simulator’s decode-phase power (measured power-flat at ≈1,268 W per GPU). The custom variants charge the LPDDR gather with the in-situ-validated window-credited law (a=0.2 µs, b=512 GB/s achieved scatter). No KV offload or eviction anywhere.

7.4 · SLO-constrained goodput

Goodput = throughput that counts only while the per-user promise holds: for a TPOT SLO, each variant’s goodput is its best ladder point with TPOT ≤ SLO. The baseline’s ladder is capacity-truncated (b=14 / b=1), so it cannot convert SLO relaxation into throughput; the custom variants can — hence the growing gap.

7.5 · Iso-throughput energy (same service, smaller bill)

Why this metric: decode power is flat per GPU, so plain iso-J/token degenerates into per-GPU throughput, and comparing J/token at max batch conflates different service levels. The fair energy question is the operator’s: to deliver exactly the baseline’s service (its max-batch throughput), what does each system burn? A variant qualifies only if some ladder point reaches the target; its power is N×1,268 W and its energy per token at the target is N×1,268/target.
