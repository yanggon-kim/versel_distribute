DeepSeek-V3.2 — Part XI: Custom-HBM LPDDR Extension, Honestly Charged

The custom-HBM design point specified end-to-end: each GPU’s 8 HBM stacks carry a base die fronting a 128 GB/s × 128 GB LPDDR extension (per GPU: 1,024 GB/s wire aggregate, 1,024 GB capacity, 1,216 GB total). Latent KV lives in LPDDR and is gathered through the base die at an assumed 0.5 wire efficiency — b = 512 GB/s achieved scatter, a = 0.2 µs — charged with the measured, in-situ-validated window-credited tier law from Part X. Three GPU-only variants (no KV offload or eviction), batch ladders under each max fit, four context lengths, in LLMServingSim over measured B200 kernels.

Part IX evaluated this capacity design with LPDDR idealized to HBM bandwidth. Part X measured what the gather actually costs and how much of it hides. This page closes the loop: the same three-way comparison, with the LPDDR gather charged by the validated law at the user-specified engine spec. The capacity verdict survives — and the honest model reveals structure the idealization could not: a throughput-optimal batch below the capacity ceiling at long context, and a bandwidth tax that matters most exactly where batches are largest.

§2Method — validated pieces only

  • EP-rank proxy: one simulated tp1 rank stands for each DP=EP rank; cluster batch = N×b, cluster throughput = N×rank. TPOT at equal per-rank batch is GPU-count-invariant (measured: MoE latency flat in expert count).
  • The lpddr512 patch: only the 115 pure-decode attention rows change: t_att += max( floor(N), a + N/b − W(n) ) − 2·δ_kernel(n) with N = n·2048·656 B (latent gather bytes only), W = 2·fusedk_B (measured overlap window), floor = 0.67 + N/44,642·10³ µs (measured HBM exposure floor), 2·δ_kernel = measured HBM exposed gather (subtracted to avoid double counting). Weights, indexer scan, top-k, MoE, dense: untouched measured values. The window-credited law reproduced 8 in-situ PCIe layer measurements to ≤0.28% (Part X §14).
  • Steady state: staggered-output workloads; steady TPOT = the last-prefilled request’s TPOT over its final 64 tokens at full concurrency; validity gate n_eff = b at the steady-window midpoint (50/50 cells pass); decode power = tail plateau of the per-second power series; TTFT = first request’s single-prefill latency.
  • Batch ladder: max fit + descending powers of two (≥8; down to 1 when max ≤ 8) — the menu between throughput-max and latency-min.
  • Anchors: the stock cells reproduce the Part IX runs — (14, 128K) +0.01%, (28, 64K) −0.02%.

§5Findings

  • 1 · The capacity win survives honest charging and grows with context: 2.23× (64K) → 8.55× (1M) for 8-GPU custom vs 8-GPU stock, identically for energy per token. Six custom GPUs beat eight stock ones at every context (1.66–6.28×) with 25% less silicon.
  • 2 · NEW: the throughput-optimal batch sits below the capacity fit at long context. C8 at 512K peaks at b=32 (5,191 tok/s) not at its b=44 fit (5,034); same for C6 (3,895 @ 32 vs 3,772 @ 43). The LPDDR gather tax grows ~linearly in batch while marginal throughput grows sublinearly — past the crossover, one more session costs more TPOT than it adds tokens. The flat-BW idealization (Part IX) never showed this: there, more batch was always better. Consequence: at long context, capacity beyond b* buys admission headroom and QoS slack, not throughput.
  • 3 · The iso-latency menu (custom at ≈ stock’s max-batch TPOT): 128K — C8 b=16: 4,693 tok/s at 27.3 ms = 1.10× stock at +4% TPOT; 512K — C8 b=8: 2,264 at 28.3 ms = 2.27× at +17%; 1M — C8 b=8: 1,862 at 34.4 ms = 5.4× at +49%. Only at 64K is iso-latency custom merely at parity (1.05×) — short context is where stock is least capacity-starved. The design does not force the max-batch latency price; it offers it.
  • 4 · The max-batch latency price: 4.4–5.6× stock’s TPOT (users read 6.5–17 tok/s vs stock’s 36–46). The ladders in §4 are the negotiation range.
  • 5 · TTFT is variant-invariant (3.4 / 7.3 / 40.8 / 143 s at 64K/128K/512K/1M) by construction — prefill is unpatched. A real LPDDR tier could slow prefill KV writes; unmodeled (§6).
  • 6 · The cost of honesty shrinks with context: vs Part IX’s flat-BW numbers, lpddr512 gives back 23% (64K), 15% (128K), 5% (512K), 2% (1M) of max-batch throughput. Gather traffic scales with batch only, while scan/attend grow with kv — so the bandwidth assumption matters most exactly where batches are largest.
  • 7 · GPU count stays a linear knob: C8 and C6 TPOT agree at equal per-rank batch to ≤0.05 ms across the grid (the N-invariance check under the patch); C6 = 6/8 of C8’s throughput at equal b.

§6Honesty box — what these numbers assume

b = 512 GB/s is an assumed engine spec (0.5 of the 1,024 GB/s wire), not a measurement. Measured reference efficiencies for scattered 656-B gathers: TMA-copy engine ~34% of pins, transfer-only ceiling 63–74%, PCIe production kernel 16.6% of its link. 0.5 sits between DMA-copy class and the ceiling — it requires the deep-pipeline base-die engine (k_eff → 1). a = 0.2 µs is aggressive vs the measured HBM head (0.67 µs/step). The Ramulator base-die model is the tool that must certify both.

  • Keys-in-HBM flag: the indexer scan is unpatched (keys assumed HBM-resident). Cells where b × keys/session exceeds free HBM overstate this: C8 — 64K b≥256, 128K b≥128, 512K b≥32, 1M b≥16; C6 (less free HBM) — 64K b≥128, 128K b≥64, 512K b≥16, 1M b≥8. Long-context max-batch rows are therefore optimistic; the iso-latency mid-ladder rows are largely clean.
  • Decode-only patch: prefill sparse attention and KV writes keep measured HBM values (hence the variant-invariant TTFT).
  • EP all-to-all unmodeled (sub-percent at NVLink5); C6 uses the 43-expert heavy-rank proxy (≤2% conservative).
  • Dummy-weight kernel profiles; 1-layer probe lineage — same basis as Parts VI–X.
